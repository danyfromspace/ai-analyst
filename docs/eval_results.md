## E2 Detection curve (one monitored listing with a $2,000+ base shocked, everything else as delivered)

| Shock | x0.1 | x0.3 | x0.5 | x0.7 | x1.5 | x2.0 | x3.0 | x5.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flagged | 76% | 37% | 16% | 5% | 8% | 21% | 42% | 68% |
| n | 240 | 240 | 240 | 240 | 240 | 240 | 240 | 240 |

Near-total losses are caught (x0.1: 76%); partial moves mostly are not (x0.5: 16%, x2: 21%, x3: 42%). Listings in this data swing that much on their own promotions, so a monthly monitor with no promotion calendar cannot tell a 3x price error from a sale. That is the monitor's minimum detectable effect on monthly data, and the main reason the next version needs weekly data with a promotion calendar.

## How to read this

- The planted errors and the checks were written by the same author. E4 shows the checks catch the families they were designed for; errors nobody anticipated are untested.
- Halcyon is AI-generated evaluation data. Real storefront statements add currency, bundles, restatements and fees.
- These evals cover the deterministic layer. Model behaviour (framing, wording, routing) is covered by the hand-run cases in `evals/agent_cases.yaml`.
